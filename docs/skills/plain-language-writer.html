What it does

Use it to

  • Make text plainer
  • Rewrite for students
  • Edit a course page or syllabus
  • Draft an email to students or faculty
  • Remove wording that sounds like AI

You bring

  • Text to edit or notes to draft from
  • Who will read it
  • Where it is going, such as an email, course page or journal
  • School style rules, if any
the skill

You get

  • Rewritten text
  • Before-and-after table of the main changes
  • List of unclear phrases to check
  • A final check of length, names, claims and terms against the original
The description the AI reads

Use when an educator asks to write, rewrite or edit text so it is clear and sounds human, not like AI. Triggers include "make this plainer", "this sounds like AI", "rewrite for students", "make this easier to follow", "edit my course page", "write an email to my students", "clean up this syllabus text".

Why you can trust it

Every rule in this skill is labelled with what it rests on: Research Standard Practice. Rules labelled Practice are local conventions your institution may change.

Limits

Plain language guidance is mostly written for public and government documents, not teaching text. The research on AI vocabulary counts how often words appear in published papers. It does not test whether removing them makes text clearer. The habits that sound like AI change as AI tools change, so the phrase list needs regular updates.
