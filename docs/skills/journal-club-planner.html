What it does

Use it to

  • I'm presenting at journal club
  • Help me understand this paper
  • How should we split the figures
  • Help me make my journal club slides
  • What questions will they ask me
  • Help me lead the discussion

You bring

  • The paper, as a PDF or full text
  • The student's own reading of the paper and each figure
  • Whether they present alone or in a group, and how much time they have
  • Who the audience is
  • The course rubric or instructions, if any
the skill

You get

  • Feedback on the student's reading of each figure
  • A plan for splitting the paper among presenters
  • A slide outline with a title for each figure slide and timing
  • Four to six discussion questions for the student to lead
  • The hard questions they are most likely to get
  • A check against the course rubric
The description the AI reads

Helps a student prepare to present a research paper at journal club. Use when a student says "I'm presenting at journal club", "help me understand this paper", "how should we split the figures", "help me make my journal club slides", "what questions will they ask me" or "help me lead the discussion". For graduate, MD-PhD, medical students, residents and postdocs, presenting alone or in a group. It helps the student understand and judge each figure in their own words, plan the talk, prepare discussion questions and get ready for hard questions. Uses only what is in the paper and never reproduces the paper itself.

Why you can trust it

Every rule in this skill is labelled with what it rests on: Research Standard Practice. Rules labelled Practice are local conventions your institution may change.

The rules and their sources

Limits

Most research on journal clubs comes from clinical training and evidence-based medicine, not graduate science training. The reviews also find that studies of journal clubs are often small. Programs should check how well the format works for their own students.
