Reading Material Generator

Turns papers and chapters into session-sized interactive readings with glossary, reflection prompts and self-checks.

MaturityTested at origin

When to use it

Turns one or more source documents (journal papers, book chapters, reviews, protocols) into structured, interactive reading material sized for a real class session in medical or biomedical-research education. Use whenever an educator asks for reading material, a pre-reading, a group reading assignment, a flipped-classroom reading, or wants to turn a paper or chapter into something learners read before or during a lecture, PBL, RBL or workshop — even without the phrase "reading material". Triggers include "turn this paper into something my students can read", "I need the reading part of my session", "make a group reading out of this chapter", "summarize this paper for a 2-hour session". Also use to shorten or lengthen an existing reading, add a glossary, add discussion questions, or add figures from the source.

Use it to

  • Create a pre-reading
  • Turn a paper into reading material
  • Group reading for a workshop

You bring

You get

Pedagogical basis

What each rule rests on. Research Standard Practice — rules labelled Practice are local conventions your institution may change. All principles and references.

Rule in this skill Basis Source
Rebuild the source as a teaching arc; depth over coverage Research Cognitive load — Sweller, 1988, Cognitive Science 12:257–285
Define every term at first use, for the least specialized reader Research Reduces extraneous load for mixed-background groups (cognitive load theory)
Higher-order reflection prompts placed right after the concept Standard Constructive alignment with objectives — Biggs, 1996; levels — Anderson & Krathwohl, 2001
Easy click-to-reveal self-check Research Retrieval practice — Roediger & Karpicke, 2006, Psychological Science 17:249–255; Larsen, Butler & Roediger, 2008, Medical Education 42:959–966
Real figures only, cited; never invent sources Standard Academic integrity; AAMC AI Principles (2025): transparency
Reading budget of 3,000 words per hour Practice Adults read non-fiction silently at ~238 words/minute (Brysbaert, 2019, Journal of Memory and Language 109:104047). The budget (~50 words/minute) deliberately leaves time for study, reflection and discussion. Institutions may measure and set their own constant
