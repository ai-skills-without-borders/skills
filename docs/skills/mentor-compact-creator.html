The description the AI reads

Builds mentoring compacts in two layers. A program sets up its shared compact once, with its expectations, milestones, funding, safety rules, time away and contacts. Then each mentor and trainee add their own agreements on top, like how often they meet, authorship, working hours and career goals. Use it when a program wants a compact template, when a new trainee joins a lab, when a mentor and trainee want to reset expectations or when a compact is due for its yearly review.

Why you can trust it

Every rule in this skill is labelled with what it rests on: Research Standard Practice. Rules labelled Practice are local conventions your institution may change.

The rules and their sources
Rule in this skill Basis Source
Mentor and trainee set out their expectations in writing early Standard AAMC Compact Between Biomedical Graduate Students and Their Research Advisors; AAMC Compact Between Postdoctoral Appointees and Their Mentors
Aligning expectations is a core part of effective mentoring Standard National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine, The Science of Effective Mentorship in STEMM, 2019
Each person answers on their own, then the pair compares and talks it through Practice Adapted from the aligning expectations activities in the Entering Mentoring curriculum (Pfund, Branchaw and Handelsman, 2015)
Mentors trained to align expectations and communicate improve their mentoring skills Research Pfund et al., 2014, randomized controlled trial of mentor training in Academic Medicine
The training and career plan is part of the compact Standard NIH policy on Individual Development Plans for graduate students and postdocs (NOT-OD-14-113, 2014)
A program layer shared by every pair, plus a pair layer each mentor and trainee write themselves Practice Based on the Mount Sinai MSTP mentor-mentee compact, which pairs program-wide expectations with space for each pair's own notes
Keep the pair layer to about a page and review it every 6 to 12 months Practice Experience of program directors who use compacts
Personal information is included only if the person wants it. Accommodations go through official channels Standard U.S. Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (FERPA) for student records; institutional accommodation policies
The compact is a draft reviewed by both people Standard AAMC Principles for the Responsible Use of AI in Medical Education, 2025

Limits

The research supports mentor training that includes aligning expectations. There is less direct evidence that a written compact by itself improves outcomes. Its value depends on the pair actually talking it through and coming back to it.

Programs outside the United States should replace FERPA and NIH policies with their own.
