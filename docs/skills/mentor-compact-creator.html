Mentor Compact Creator

Builds a two-layer mentoring compact: a shared program layer written once, and a one-page agreement each mentor and trainee add on top.

MaturityDraft
Version0.1.0
Used byEducators, Learners and program staff
Runs codeNo
Maintained byTalia H. Swartz, MD, PhD (Icahn School of Medicine at Mount Sinai)

When to use it

Builds mentoring compacts in two layers. A program sets up its shared compact once, with its expectations, milestones, funding, safety rules, time away and contacts. Then each mentor and trainee add their own agreements on top, like how often they meet, authorship, working hours and career goals. Use it when a program wants a compact template, when a new trainee joins a lab, when a mentor and trainee want to reset expectations or when a compact is due for its yearly review.

Use it to

  • Create a program mentoring compact
  • Set expectations with a new trainee
  • Compare mentor and trainee expectations
  • Reset expectations with a trainee
  • Review a compact yearly

You bring

  • The program's compact, handbook or policies
  • Mentor's and trainee's separate answers
  • The trainee's stage
  • The current compact, for an update
the skill

You get

  • Program compact, marked program rule or pair addition
  • Side-by-side comparison of mentor and trainee answers
  • Neutral questions for differences
  • One-page pair agreement
  • Review date

Pedagogical basis

What each rule rests on. Research Standard Practice — rules labelled Practice are local conventions your institution may change. All principles and references.

| Rule in this skill | Basis | Source | |---|---|---| | Mentor and trainee set out their expectations in writing early | Standard | AAMC Compact Between Biomedical Graduate Students and Their Research Advisors; AAMC Compact Between Postdoctoral Appointees and Their Mentors | | Aligning expectations is a core part of effective mentoring | Standard | National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine, The Science of Effective Mentorship in STEMM, 2019 | | Each person answers on their own, then the pair compares and talks it through | Practice | Adapted from the aligning expectations activities in the Entering Mentoring curriculum (Pfund, Branchaw and Handelsman, 2015) | | Mentors trained to align expectations and communicate improve their mentoring skills | Research | Pfund et al., 2014, randomized controlled trial of mentor training in Academic Medicine | | The training and career plan is part of the compact | Standard | NIH policy on Individual Development Plans for graduate students and postdocs (NOT-OD-14-113, 2014) | | A program layer shared by every pair, plus a pair layer each mentor and trainee write themselves | Practice | Based on the Mount Sinai MSTP mentor-mentee compact, which pairs program-wide expectations with space for each pair's own notes | | Keep the pair layer to about a page and review it every 6 to 12 months | Practice | Experience of program directors who use compacts | | Personal information is included only if the person wants it. Accommodations go through official channels | Standard | U.S. Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (FERPA) for student records; institutional accommodation policies | | The compact is a draft reviewed by both people | Standard | AAMC Principles for the Responsible Use of AI in Medical Education, 2025 |

Limits

The research supports mentor training that includes aligning expectations. There is less direct evidence that a written compact by itself improves outcomes. Its value depends on the pair actually talking it through and coming back to it.

Programs outside the United States should replace FERPA and NIH policies with their own.
