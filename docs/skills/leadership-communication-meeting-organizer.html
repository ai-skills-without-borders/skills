What it does

Use it to

  • Help me run a speaking practice session
  • Plan a leadership communication workshop
  • Assign meeting roles for a communication session
  • Write impromptu speaking prompts for medical students
  • Make role guides for a timekeeper and grammarian
  • Write a prep sheet for students coming to a communication session

You bring

  • Number of people and their stage, such as first-year medical students or residents
  • Session length and whether it's in person or virtual
  • What the group should practice, such as leadership communication or explaining science simply
  • Who runs the meeting and who leads impromptu speaking
  • Whether anyone will give a prepared speech
  • Any existing slides, sign-up sheet or role guides
the skill

You get

  • A run of show with segments and timing
  • A sign-up sheet and name and role cards
  • A one-page guide for each role
  • A prep sheet for participants
  • 15 to 20 impromptu speaking prompts suited to the group
  • Guidance for evaluators, end-of-meeting reports and the group reflection
  • A facilitator guide and slide outline
  • Adjustments for virtual, large-group, short or repeated sessions
The description the AI reads

Helps faculty or student leaders organize and run a structured meeting where everyone takes a role, speaks and gives feedback to practice leadership communication. Use when someone says "help me run a speaking practice session", "plan a communication workshop where students practice speaking", "assign meeting roles for a public speaking session", "write impromptu speaking prompts", "make role guides for a timekeeper and grammarian" or "help me run a leadership communication session". It produces the run of show, role assignments, role guides, student prep sheets, impromptu prompts, feedback guidance and printable materials. It works for in-person, virtual, short or large-group sessions.

Why you can trust it

Every rule in this skill is labelled with what it rests on: Research Standard Practice. Rules labelled Practice are local conventions your institution may change.

The rules and their sources
Rule in this skill Basis Source
People improve by speaking repeatedly with immediate, specific feedback Research Ericsson, 2004
Feedback names a specific strength and a specific next step, about the task, not the person Research Hattie and Timperley, 2007
Feedback is timely, specific, given in a safe setting and ends with a plan Standard Ramani and Krackov, 2012
Low-stakes setting, ground rules and normalizing nerves Practice Leaders of speaking practice groups
Rotating listening and speaking roles so everyone takes part Practice Structured speaking practice meetings
Prompts avoid private or sensitive topics and anyone can pass Practice Educators running group sessions
