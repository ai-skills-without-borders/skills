What it does

Use it to

  • Help me plan my rotations
  • Which lab should I join
  • What should I ask a PI
  • Compare the labs I rotated in
  • How do I ask to join a lab

You bring

  • Where the student is in choosing
  • The program's rules on rotations, deadlines and approvals
  • The student's interests and limits
  • Labs under consideration, if any
the skill

You get

  • The student's ranked priorities for a lab
  • A rotation plan with a question each rotation should answer
  • Questions for the PI, lab members and program leaders
  • Reflection on what each rotation showed
  • A side-by-side comparison of labs against the student's priorities
  • A plan for asking to join and confirming funding and expectations
The description the AI reads

Helps a graduate or MD-PhD student plan rotations and choose a thesis lab. It helps the student work out what they need from a lab, plan rotations, prepare questions for the PI and lab members, reflect on what they saw during a rotation, compare labs against their own priorities and ask to join. The student makes the decision.

Why you can trust it

Every rule in this skill is labelled with what it rests on: Research Standard Practice. Rules labelled Practice are local conventions your institution may change.

The rules and their sources
Rule in this skill Basis Source
The relationship with the mentor matters a great deal to a student's progress and well-being, so mentoring gets close attention Research National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine, 2019
Good mentorship and a supportive PI are linked to lower anxiety and depression in graduate students, so lab culture and support are part of the choice Research Evans et al., 2018
The student works out their own priorities, talks to current and former students and asks the PI directly about mentoring, funding and expectations Standard Jabre et al., 2021, Ten simple rules for choosing a PhD supervisor
Expectations are put in writing after the student joins Standard AAMC Compact Between Biomedical Graduate Students and Their Research Advisors
MD-PhD questions about the PI's experience with MD-PhD students, the shorter timeline, F30 support and a physician-scientist on the committee Practice MD-PhD program guidance, for example the University of Pennsylvania MSTP student guide to choosing rotation and thesis mentors
The student decides and the AI does not rank labs or people Standard AAMC Principles for the Responsible Use of AI in Medical Education, 2025
Program rules and deadlines come first Practice Each program's handbook

Limits

Most research on mentoring looks at the relationship once it exists, not at how students choose a lab. The advice on choosing comes mostly from published guides and program experience. Programs should adapt the questions and timelines to their own rules.
