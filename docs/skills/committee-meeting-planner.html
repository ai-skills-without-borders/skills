Committee Meeting Planner

Helps a graduate student plan, run and write up their own thesis committee meeting, from the scheduling poll to the summary of the committee's advice.

MaturityDraft
Version0.1.0
Used byLearners
Runs codeNo
Maintained byTalia H. Swartz, MD, PhD (Icahn School of Medicine at Mount Sinai)

When to use it

Helps a graduate student plan and run their own thesis committee meeting from start to finish. Use when a student says "I need to schedule my committee meeting", "help me write to my committee", "follow up on my poll", "send the calendar invite", "what should I put in my committee slides", "help me write my progress report" or "help me write up my committee meeting". Drafts every email and the calendar invite, helps prepare the slides and progress report and writes the summary afterward. Nothing is sent without the student's approval.

Use it to

  • Schedule a thesis committee meeting
  • Write to the committee
  • Follow up on a scheduling poll
  • Prepare committee slides
  • Write a progress report
  • Write up the committee's advice

You bring

  • The program's rules for committee meetings
  • Committee members, emails and time zones
  • Date and action items of the last meeting
  • Poll responses
  • The student's notes and data
  • The student's notes from the meeting
the skill

You get

  • Poll email in every member's time zone
  • Reminders and confirmation emails
  • Calendar invite
  • Slide outline
  • Progress report draft (2–3 pages)
  • Materials email and day-before reminder
  • Meeting agenda
  • One-page summary of the committee's advice

Pedagogical basis

What each rule rests on. Research Standard Practice — rules labelled Practice are local conventions your institution may change. All principles and references.

Rule in this skill Basis Source
Regular committee meetings, with progress, problems and a plan Practice Common graduate program policy. Programs set their own schedule
The student organizes the meeting and comes with questions Practice Many programs make the student responsible for their committee meetings, as part of learning to manage their own research
Training and career plans are part of the meeting Standard NIH asks that grantees describe the use of Individual Development Plans for graduate students and postdocs in annual progress reports (NOT-OD-14-113, 2014)
The student checks with the mentor before writing to the committee and shares materials with them first Practice Experienced mentors and program directors
The student writes up the advice and asks the committee to confirm it Practice Common practice for keeping students on track and avoiding misunderstandings
Present work honestly, including what failed; never invent or overstate results Standard Research integrity standards for trainees, such as NIH's requirement for training in the responsible conduct of research
Personal information stays out of what goes to the committee Standard U.S. Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (FERPA) for student records; AAMC Principles for the Responsible Use of AI in Medical Education, 2025 (protect privacy)
Nothing is sent without the student's approval; AI drafts are reviewed Standard AAMC Principles for the Responsible Use of AI in Medical Education, 2025
8 to 12 poll slots, five working days to answer, personal reminders Practice Experience of students and program coordinators. Change to fit your program

Limits

Almost all of this skill is local practice, not tested research. That is normal for this kind of task. The best evidence will come from students and programs that use it and report back, for example whether meetings happen on time more often and whether students find them more useful.

Programs outside the United States should replace FERPA and NIH policies with their own.
