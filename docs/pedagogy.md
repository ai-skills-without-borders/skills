# Pedagogical foundations

Every rule in these skills should be traceable: to research, to a professional standard, or — honestly labelled — to experienced local practice. This page shows which is which, where each principle appears in the skills, and where the evidence has limits.

**Three kinds of basis**

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Research** | Supported by experimental studies or meta-analyses |
| **Standard** | A professional consensus or published standard (AAMC, NBME, measurement theory) |
| **Practice** | A local convention from experienced educators. Useful and explainable, not directly tested. Institutions may change it |

---

## The principles

### 1. Constructive alignment — *Standard (theory widely adopted)*
Learning objectives, learning activities and assessment are designed together, so that learners practise and are assessed on exactly what the objectives say (Biggs, 1996).

**Where it appears**
- `learning-objectives-designer`: checks each objective for an activity and an assessment and reports the gaps.
- `syllabus-builder`: links objectives, schedule and assessment, and counts a competency as *evidence* only when it is assessed.
- `exam-question-writer`: a blueprint before items; one item tests one objective.
- `lesson-format-checker`: every lesson segment names the objective it serves, and every objective is served.

### 2. Observable, levelled objectives — *Standard*
Objectives state what learners will be able to do, with an observable verb, at a defined cognitive level (Anderson & Krathwohl, 2001). For skills and procedures, a condition and a criterion make them measurable (Mager, 1962).

**Where it appears**
- `learning-objectives-designer`: one verb, one target; no "understand" or "know"; level matched to what the activity demands.
- *Practice:* collapsing Bloom's six levels into three (Foundational / Applied / Advanced) is a local convention for easier agreement. The skill records both.

### 3. Competency-based education and entrustment — *Standard*
Curricula are organized around observable competencies, and trust is based on activities learners are seen to perform (ten Cate, 2005). Frameworks in use include the AAMC/AACOM/ACGME Foundational Competencies (2024) and, for physician-scientists, the AAMC GREAT MD-PhD competencies with milestones (Estrada et al., 2026). The AAMC asks that no external framework be adopted without formal faculty review.

**Where it appears**
- `syllabus-builder` and `learning-objectives-designer`: framework links are proposals for the course leader to approve; wording comes only from the supplied document.
- *Practice:* the E / P / X levels (evidence / practice / exposure) and the "re-check above six E links" rule are local methods that apply the entrustment idea — performed *and* observed — to course mapping.

### 4. Problem-based learning — *Research (mixed outcomes)*
Working on a problem activates prior knowledge, invites elaboration, and stores knowledge in the context where it will be used (Schmidt, 1983; Norman & Schmidt, 1992).

**Where it appears**
- `pbl-rbl-session-builder`: case- or tool-driven phases, generating learning issues and research questions, and student-stage calibration.
- **Limit:** reviews of whole PBL curricula show modest or mixed effects on knowledge outcomes (Colliver, 2000). The skill does not rely on PBL alone; it builds in the structure in principles 5 and 6.

### 5. Problem solving before instruction (productive failure) — *Research, with boundary conditions*
Attempting a problem before being taught the concept, then receiving instruction that builds on those attempts, improves conceptual understanding and transfer (Kapur, 2008; Schwartz & Bransford, 1998). A meta-analysis of 53 studies found a moderate advantage over instruction-first, larger when the design followed productive-failure principles — but not for the youngest learners or for domain-general skills (Sinha & Kapur, 2021).

**Where it appears**
- `pbl-rbl-session-builder`: the *productive trap* in each phase, *predict before reveal*, *design before seeing*, and *information release* (immediate / earned / reward). The reward step is the consolidation the research says is required.
- **Limit:** struggle without consolidation is just failure. The skill requires complete expected answers in the facilitator notes and a reveal after each trap.

### 6. Managing cognitive load and guidance — *Research*
Working memory is limited. Unguided problem solving can consume the capacity needed to learn (Sweller, 1988), and minimally guided instruction is less effective for novices (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006).

**Where it appears**
- `pbl-rbl-session-builder`: one inference per prompt, observation before inference, and calibration to what students cannot yet do. This is how the skill reconciles principle 5 with principle 6: struggle is designed, narrow and bounded.
- `reading-material-generator`: a teaching arc in the order a newcomer needs, inline definitions at first use, and depth over coverage.
- `lesson-format-checker`: time estimates on every part, and deliberate keep/strip choices for code (provided code at first exposure, student-written code once the pattern is known).
- `learning-objectives-designer`: one target per objective.

### 7. Retrieval practice with feedback — *Research (strong)*
Recalling information strengthens long-term retention more than restudying it (Roediger & Karpicke, 2006), including in medical education, where feedback is essential (Larsen, Butler & Roediger, 2008).

**Where it appears**
- `reading-material-generator`: a click-to-reveal self-check at the end of each reading.
- `pbl-rbl-session-builder`: comprehension quizzes answerable from the reading.
- `exam-question-writer`: in formative mode, a rationale for every option so learners get feedback.

### 8. Item-writing quality — *Standard and Research*
A validated taxonomy of 31 item-writing guidelines (Haladyna, Downing & Rodriguez, 2002) and the NBME *Item-Writing Guide* (6th ed., 2020) define well-formed items. Flaws matter: in four medical-school exams, 36–65% of items were flawed, and flawed items disadvantaged some students (Downing, 2005).

**Where it appears**
- `exam-question-writer`: the flaw checklist, the cover-the-options test, homogeneous options, no "all/none of the above", fair vignettes.

### 9. Assessment validity and purpose — *Standard (measurement theory)*
Assessment results support decisions only to the extent there is validity evidence for that use (Downing, 2003). The purpose — learning or decision — changes what matters (van der Vleuten, 1996). AAMC guidance on AI makes the same point: validity matters more for summative use, rich feedback for formative use.

**Where it appears**
- `exam-question-writer`: asks formative or summative first; summative items need expert review, piloting and item analysis.
- `syllabus-builder`: a competency counts as evidence only where performance is observed or assessed.

### 10. Responsible AI use in education — *Standard*
The AAMC *Principles for the Responsible Use of AI in and for Medical Education* (2025) call for transparency and disclosure, equal access, data privacy, and human-centred use.

**Where it appears**
- Every skill: an AI-assistance line in the institution's wording; no identifiable patient or learner data; the educator approves before anything is final; no invented sources.
- The library: open format and paste-in versions so access does not depend on one paid tool.

## Local practice, labelled honestly

These rules come from teaching experience in the MD-PhD program at the Gutwirth Medical School. They are explainable but not directly tested. Institutions may change them.

| Rule | Skill | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Reading budget of 3,000 words per hour | reading-material-generator | Adults read non-fiction silently at about 238 words per minute (Brysbaert, 2019). The budget of ~50 words per minute deliberately leaves time for studying, reflecting and discussing. Measure your own learners if you can |
| Three cognitive levels instead of six | learning-objectives-designer | Easier agreement between faculty; Bloom level is still recorded |
| E / P / X evidence levels; re-check above six E links | syllabus-builder | Guards against inflated competency maps |
| 2–4 objectives per session, 3–5 per course | objectives, syllabus | Keeps alignment checkable |
| Facilitator voice, invisible pedagogy, human texture, wonder | pbl-rbl-session-builder | Craft guidance for writing cases; consistent with the research above, not separately tested |
| Format rules (box colours, glossary as table, code markers) | lesson-format-checker | Operational: consistency and compatibility with grading and export tools |

## Known debates and limits

- **Discovery vs. guidance.** Principles 5 and 6 pull in different directions. The skills take the position supported by the meta-analytic evidence: designed, bounded struggle followed by explicit consolidation, calibrated to learners' prior knowledge.
- **AI-generated content needs checking.** No skill claims that its output is valid. Exam items, maps and readings are drafts for expert review.
- **Evidence from general education.** Several findings (productive failure, retrieval practice) come mostly from school and university settings. Medical-education evidence exists for retrieval practice; for others, institutions should collect their own data. The library's usage reports are one way to do that.

## How to propose a change

If your experience or a study contradicts a rule here, open an issue with the source. Rules labelled **Practice** are the first candidates for change.
