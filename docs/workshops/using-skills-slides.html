Part 1

What a skill is

Using AI skills4 / 19
Speaker notesFifteen minutes of explanation and a live demonstration, then it's your turn.

A one-off prompt and a skill

A one-off prompt

  • Written from scratch each time, by each person
  • Quality depends on how well you prompted today
  • Lost when the chat ends
  • Hard to share, review or improve

A skill

  • Written once by someone who knows the task well
  • Same standards and steps every time, for everyone
  • Installed once, available in every chat
  • Shared, tested at other institutions, improved over time

Using AI skills6 / 19
Speaker notesThink of a skill as a protocol for the AI. It holds what an expert knows and how the expert works. You don't run it; you ask in plain words and the assistant uses it when the task fits.

What happens behind the scenes

  1. You ask in plain words, with your material attached
  2. It matches the task to the skill's description
  3. It follows the framework, asking for what's missing
  4. It draws on the knowledge when a step needs it
  5. You approve at checkpoints, and decide

Speaker notesYou don't need to name the skill. If the assistant doesn't use it, say "use the skill" and its name. Halfway through, check that everyone has a first output.

Review before you use it

  • Correct? Facts, numbers, dates, terms against your source
  • Invented anything? References, page numbers, policies
  • Fits? Your learners, your course, your rules
  • As promised? Compare with the skill page's expected output
  • What would you change? Write it down
