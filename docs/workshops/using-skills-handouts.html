Handouts: Using AI skills

Everything participants need on paper, in one page. Print it from your browser, or share the link.

The full plan

Before the session (15 minutes, at home)

  1. Find out which AI tool and plan you haveIf your institution provides an AI assistant, ask your IT or AI office which one and which plan (for example ChatGPT Edu, Claude for Education, Gemini or Microsoft 365 Copilot), and whether skills are switched on. If you use a personal account, the plan is shown in your account settings.
  2. If you have no AI account, open a free oneFollow your institution's policy on which tools you may use. When choosing a free plan, it helps to know that as of November 2026 Claude's free plan lets you upload skills, while the free plans of ChatGPT and Gemini do not. Every skill in the library also has a paste-in version that works in any assistant, free plans included, so you will be able to take part with any of them.
  3. Sign in on the laptop you will bringOpen the assistant in your browser, sign in, and check that you can start a new chat. Write down your password if you rarely use it. Sessions often lose their first minutes to forgotten passwords and two-factor codes on a phone left at home.
  4. Choose one documentPick something from your own teaching that you would like help with: objectives to rewrite, a session to plan, a paper to turn into a reading, exam questions to check. It must contain nothing confidential: no patient data, no student names or grades, nothing unpublished you may not share.
  5. Look at the library (optional)Open the skills catalog and note one or two skills that match your document. Don't install anything yet; we will do it together.

Install a skill, step by step

First download the skill. On the skill's page in the catalog, click Download ZIP. Don't unzip it: the AI tool wants the ZIP file as it is. Then follow the steps for your tool. The steps follow each tool's help pages as of November 2026. Menu names change often; if a step doesn't match what you see, look for the word Skills.

Claude (claude.ai or the Claude app)

  1. Open Settings → Capabilities and make sure Code execution and file creation is on. Skills need it.
  2. Open Customize → Skills.
  3. Click +, then Create skill, then Upload a skill, and choose the ZIP you downloaded.
  4. Uploaded skills start switched off. Find the skill in the list and switch it on.
  5. On a Team or Enterprise plan, an owner must first enable skills for the organization. If you don't see Skills, ask your administrator, and use the paste-in version today.

ChatGPT (Business, Enterprise, Edu)

  1. In the sidebar open Plugins, then the Skills tab. On some plans Skills appears directly in the sidebar.
  2. Click Create, then Upload from your computer, and choose the ZIP.
  3. ChatGPT scans the skill before it becomes available; this usually takes a minute or two.
  4. On Enterprise and Edu, skills are off until an administrator turns them on. On Free and Plus there are no skills: use the paste-in version.

Gemini

  1. Gemini Enterprise: open Skills, click +, then Upload, and choose the ZIP.
  2. The regular Gemini app: use the paste-in version as the instructions of a Gem (see below).

Microsoft 365 Copilot

  1. Unzip the skill. Put the skill's folder in your OneDrive under Documents/Cowork/Skills/.
  2. Start a new Cowork conversation. Both Cowork and Copilot Studio need a Microsoft 365 Copilot license.

Any assistant, without installing

  1. On the skill's page, under Try it now, without installing, click Copy the skill. It copies the instructions and all reference files in one go.
  2. Quickest: open a new chat, paste, and write your request at the end of the message.
  3. To keep it: paste the same text into the instructions of a custom GPT, a Gem or a Project.
  4. The paste-in version does the same job. It cannot run scripts, which only one skill in the library uses, and that skill still works by reading.

Practice material

The question used in the demonstration, and a second one for participants whose own document doesn't match any skill. Use them with Exam Question Writer.

Demonstration question
Which of the following is NOT a function of the kidney?
A. Regulation of blood pressure
B. Production of erythropoietin
C. It produces bile, which is stored in the gallbladder and released into the duodenum to help digest fats
D. All of the above

Practice question
A 54-year-old man is started on an ACE inhibitor. Which of the following is an adverse effect?
A. Hyperkalaemia
B. A persistent dry cough, caused by the accumulation of bradykinin, which is not broken down when ACE is inhibited
C. Hypoglycaemia
D. Both A and B

Use the skill on your material

  1. Start a new chatA skill is picked up in a new conversation. With the paste-in version, start in the GPT, Gem or Project you made, or paste the text first.
  2. Ask in plain words and attach your documentFor example: "Rewrite the learning objectives in this session outline", with the outline attached. You don't have to name the skill. If the assistant doesn't seem to use it, say "Use the Learning Objectives Designer skill".
  3. Answer its questionsA good skill asks for what it needs (your learners' stage, a framework, a length) instead of guessing. Answer briefly; you can say you don't know.
  4. Approve or correct at each checkpointThe skill stops to show you its plan or a first part. Read it before you agree. This is where your judgement shapes the result.

Review checklist

  • Is it correct? Check facts, numbers, dates and terminology against your source.
  • Did it invent anything? References, page numbers, policies, quotations. The library's skills are told never to invent sources, but check every one.
  • Does it fit? Your learners' level, your course, your institution's rules.
  • Did it do what the skill page promised? Compare with the expected output.
  • What would you change before using it? Write it down; it goes in your report.
