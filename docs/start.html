A skilltransferable know-how any assistant can use

A skill is transferable teaching know-how, packaged for an AI assistant. It is a short set of written instructions that an educator wrote once, so that anyone's AI assistant can do one teaching task the way an experienced colleague would.

Think of it as a protocol for the AI. A good protocol holds two things: knowledge (the standards, rules and common mistakes an expert knows) and a framework (the steps, the checkpoints where you approve, and the exact shape of the result). A skill holds both.

You don't open a skill or run it. Once it is installed, you ask your assistant in ordinary words, for example "write exam questions for my renal objectives". The assistant recognizes the task, loads the right skill, and follows it.

Prompt or skill: the difference

A one-off prompt

  • Written from scratch each time, by each person
  • Quality depends on how well you prompted today
  • Lost when the chat ends
  • Hard to share, review or improve

A skill

  • Written once by someone who knows the task well
  • Same standards and steps every time, for everyone
  • Installed once, available in every chat
  • Shared, tested at other schools, and improved over time

How to use a skill

  1. Install itDownload a skill from the catalog and upload it in your AI tool's skills settings. No skills in your plan? Use the paste-in version.
  2. Ask in plain wordsDescribe your task and attach your material, for example your slides, a paper, or your objectives. The skill asks for anything it still needs.
  3. Review and decideThe skill stops for your approval at key points, flags what it couldn't verify, and never invents sources. You stay the expert.

Why use skills

Expertise travels

What one experienced educator knows about writing objectives or exam items reaches colleagues in other schools and countries, without a workshop.

Consistent quality

Every course leader gets the same checks: one target per objective, no flawed exam items, timings that add up.

Less time on the mechanics

The routine parts get done quickly, leaving more of your time for teaching decisions.

Not locked to one tool

Skills use an open format. Your institution can switch AI providers without losing the work.

Is a skill just a long prompt?

Partly. Unlike a prompt, it is saved and loaded automatically when it fits, it can carry reference files and examples, and it is versioned, tested and shared like any other teaching resource.

Does a skill send my material anywhere?

No. A skill is only instructions. Your material goes wherever your AI tool already sends it, so follow your institution's rules for that tool, and never share identifiable patient or student data.

Do I need to know how to code?

No. Only one skill here has optional scripts, and it still works without them. Writing a new skill means writing clear instructions in plain English.

Can I change a skill for my school?

Yes. Skills are openly licensed. Adapt the wording, your framework or your templates, and consider sharing the improvement back.

What happens behind the scenes

You talk to your assistant as usual. The skill works in the background.

  1. You ask"Write exam items for my renal objectives", with your material attached.
  2. It matchesThe assistant recognizes the task from the skill's description.
  3. It follows the frameworkSteps in order, asking you for what's missing.
  4. It draws on the knowledgeStandards and checklists, only when a step needs them.
  5. You approveIt stops at checkpoints, flags gaps, and you decide.
