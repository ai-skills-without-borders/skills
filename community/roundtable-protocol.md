# Round-table workshop protocol

> The two full workshops, with lesson plans, slides and handouts, are on the [Workshops page](https://ai-skills-without-borders.github.io/skills/workshops.html). This is the shorter 75-minute round-table version for conferences.

A 75-minute hands-on workshop in which educators build an AI skill for a real need, test whether a colleague can use it, and add it to the shared library. It suits graduate, MD-PhD and postdoctoral training leaders, faculty educators and program administrators at any level of AI experience. No programming experience is needed.

The workshop also tests the library itself: can a skill written by one educator be used, adapted and trusted by another, in another AI tool or institution?

## Learning objectives

Participants will be able to:

1. Recognize the value of reusable AI skills for global collaboration and for sharing educational resources across AI platforms, institutions and users.
2. Apply an AI skill to an unmet need in biomedical research education, training or program administration.

## Before the workshop

- **Ask participants to bring** a laptop, internet access, an active account with a major AI platform (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini or Copilot), and a non-confidential educational document: a reference, an assignment, a lesson, a syllabus, a rubric or similar. No patient data and no learner data.
- **Set up the tables**, one facilitator per table:
  1. Grading and assessment
  2. Curriculum and lesson development
  3. Reading materials and assignments
  4. Formatting and adapting existing educational resources
  5. Mentoring and program administration
- **Print the skill template and the recording sheet** (below), one of each per participant, or share them as a web form.
- **Prepare an example skill** from the library for each table, so participants can start from it instead of from a blank page if they prefer.

## Timing (75 minutes)

| Time | Segment |
|---|---|
| 15 min | **Introduction and demonstration.** What an AI skill is, how it differs from a prompt, and how a skill is created, customized and shared. Examples from biomedical research education, and what to consider when moving a skill between ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and other platforms |
| 5 min | **Choose a table and a task.** Participants choose a table by interest, and the facilitator helps each one pick a specific task based on the material they brought |
| 25 min | **Build the skill.** Individually or in pairs, participants fill in the skill template, write the skill, test it on their own material and refine the instructions based on the results |
| 15 min | **Peer exchange and portability test.** Participants swap skills within the table and check whether a colleague can use them. Where possible, they try a skill in a different AI platform or with another institution's material and note what had to change |
| 10 min | **Shared library and community.** Participants add their skills to the shared collection. A short discussion on how the collection grows into a community of practice: documentation, peer review, accessibility and working across institutions |
| 5 min | **Closing.** Key lessons, and how to find, adapt and contribute skills after the workshop |

## Skill template (one per skill)

Every skill built in the workshop is documented the same way, so another educator can judge and reuse it.

1. **Name and purpose.** What task the skill does, and when an educator should use it.
2. **Inputs.** What the user must provide (for example a syllabus, a paper, a list of objectives) and what the skill should ask for if it is missing.
3. **Instructions.** The steps the AI follows, the standards it applies, and the points where it stops for the educator's approval.
4. **Expected output.** The exact shape of the result: sections, format, length.
5. **Success criteria.** How you will know the output is good enough to use.
6. **Limitations.** What the skill does not do, where it may fail, and what the educator must always check.

A skill submitted to the library is checked against the same six points.

1. Table and task (one sentence):

3. AI tool and plan:
4. Minutes until the skill produced a first usable output: ___
5. Help needed: none / facilitator once / facilitator several times / could not proceed
6. Peer test: could a colleague use your skill without your help? yes / with help / no
7. Portability: tried in another AI tool? which, and what had to change:
8. What you would still change in the skill:
9. Will you use it after today? yes / maybe / no
10. May we add your skill to the shared library, and contact you about it? yes / no, email (optional):

## After the workshop

- Add the skills participants agreed to share to the library as drafts, with their authors credited.
- Enter the recording sheets as usage reports (with consent), and update each skill's "tested in" record per AI tool.
- Open an issue for each "what you would still change".
- Invite participants who answered yes to question 10 to the next community call.

## What we measure

- Number of skills developed and shared.
- Share of skills that passed the peer test, and the changes needed to move a skill between platforms.
- Brief participant feedback on usability and on how they intend to use their skill.

The headline number is not attendance but **independent reuse**: how many skills a colleague could use without the author's help, and how many participants later return a usage report or an improvement.
