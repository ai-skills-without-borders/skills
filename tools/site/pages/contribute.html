Adapt, improve and share skills

The library gets better when educators at other institutions use the skills, tell us what happened, and change them. Here is every way to take part, from five minutes to a new skill.

How contributions improve a skill

A skill is never finished. Every educator who uses it can move it forward: a usage report, a suggested change, an edit, or a review from another school. Each step raises how much the next educator can trust it.

Edit a skill directly

You don't need to know Git. Everything happens in the browser, and nothing changes in the library until it is approved.

  1. Open the skill's page and choose "Edit the skill on GitHub"You need a free GitHub account. The editor shows the skill's instructions as plain text.
  2. Make your change and choose "Propose changes"GitHub makes a copy of the library under your account (a fork) and prepares a pull request.
  3. Automatic checks runThe build checks the skill's format, links and card. If something fails, the pull request says what to fix.
  4. A maintainer approves itOne approval is enough. See who approves what.
  5. Merged and publishedThe website and downloads update automatically, and your name is in the history.

Create an institutional variant

Most adaptations don't need a copy of the skill. Try these in order.

  1. Configure itSkills take your institution's material as input: your competency framework (frameworks), your format profile, your syllabus template, your AI-use wording. Supply them in the conversation or as files.
  2. Improve the shared skillIf others would benefit, propose the change to the shared skill. Everyone gains, and you don't maintain a copy.
  3. Make an institutional variantFor what is truly local, create a variant under variants/<your-institution>/. Your institution owns and approves it, and it appears on the parent skill's page. How variants work.

Contributor guide

What makes a good skill

A real recurring task, documented in six parts: purpose, inputs, instructions, expected output, success criteria and limitations. Plain trigger words, tool-agnostic, and the evidence behind its rules.

The pedagogy

Run a contribution workshop

In 75 minutes, participants bring a non-confidential document, build a skill for a real need at a table for their area of work, test it with a colleague and in another AI tool, and add it to the shared library. The protocol has the schedule, the skill template and a recording sheet.
