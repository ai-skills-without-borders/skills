Why this project

Educators in biomedical research and medicine solve the same teaching problems in every institution, each on their own. AI skills let one educator's know-how become a tool that any colleague, anywhere, can use, adapt and improve.

The problem we start from

Every program writes learning objectives, builds sessions, prepares readings, writes and grades exams, and runs mentoring and administration. Much of the expertise behind doing these well sits with a few experienced people, and it rarely leaves their institution.

AI assistants can now help with this work, but most educators use them through one-off prompts: written from scratch each time, with quality that depends on the day, and lost when the chat ends. Many biomedical research educators have not yet seen how to package their expertise in a form that lasts and can be shared.

The idea: write once, share, improve together

An AI skill packages instructions, reference material and a step-by-step workflow into one reusable tool. It can be shared and adapted across AI platforms, institutions and users.

  1. Write it onceAn educator who knows a task well writes the skill: what it is for, what it needs, the steps, what it should produce, and its limits.
  2. Share itColleagues at other institutions use it in their own AI tool: Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini or Copilot, with a paste-in version for free plans.
  3. Improve it togetherEach use, report, adaptation and review makes the skill better and shows how far it can be trusted.

Who it is for

Leaders of graduate, MD-PhD and postdoctoral training, faculty educators, course leaders and program administrators. Medical-school educators are a large part of the community, and the library also serves biomedical research education beyond medicine. No programming experience is needed.

What educators use skills for

Five areas of work, each with the skills that serve it today. Where there is no skill yet, the first ones will come from the community.

Grading and assessment

Exam Question Writer writes and checks exam items. Grading support is still open.

Learning Objectives Designer, Syllabus Builder and PBL / RBL Session Builder.

Reading materials and assignments

Reading Material Generator turns papers and chapters into structured readings with self-checks.

Formatting and adapting existing resources

Lesson Format Checker checks lessons and notebooks against your institution's format.

Mentoring and program administration

No skill yet. Share one if you have a workflow that works.

A global community of practice

A shared library becomes a community when people agree on how skills are written, checked and shared. These are the standards we are building together.

  • Documentation. Every skill states its purpose, inputs, steps, expected outputs and limitations, and what its rules rest on. See the pedagogy.
  • Quality assurance. Maturity levels from draft to reviewed, usage reports, and peer review by educators at other institutions. See how skills are tested.
  • Portability. Each skill records which AI tools it has been tested in and what had to change, so another educator knows what to expect.
  • Accessibility. Plain language, no programming, an open format, and paste-in versions so access does not depend on one paid tool.
  • Collaborative sharing. Open licences, institutional variants, and one-maintainer approval so contributions move quickly. See how to contribute.

Hands-on workshops

Skills spread best when educators build them together. In our 75-minute round-table workshop, participants bring a non-confidential document from their institution, choose a table by area of work, and build a skill for a real need. They then swap skills with a colleague to test whether someone else can use it, try it in another AI tool where they can, and add it to the shared library. Everyone leaves with a skill built for their own needs and access to their colleagues' skills.
