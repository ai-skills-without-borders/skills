# MD-PhD Calendar Builder

This skill helps an MD-PhD program put every date it needs in one calendar: the program's yearly events, each student's milestones through both degrees and the national dates the program depends on. Each entry has an owner, an audience and a reminder with enough lead time to act.

It is built for any MD-PhD program. The program's own structure, rules and dates come first.

Why these rules: the basis for each step is in the "Why these rules" section below.

## Before you start

Ask only for what is missing:

- Program structure. The order and usual length of each phase, for example two preclinical years, a PhD of about four years and then clinical training. Note any options, such as when students take Step 1.
- Program rules. When students must choose a thesis lab, take the qualifying exam, form a committee, meet with the committee and return to clinical training.
- Cohorts. Entering years of current students and roughly how many in each phase. Use cohort years, not student names.
- What already exists. Current calendars, handbooks, the medical school and graduate school academic calendars and any lists of recurring events.
- Who maintains it, and where it will live, such as Google Calendar, Outlook or a shared spreadsheet.
- Funding. Whether the program has an NIH T32 or other grants with reporting dates.

## Steps

1. Collect what exists. Gather current calendars and rules. List every date already in use with its source.
2. Map student milestones. Use the "Student milestones by phase" section below to lay out each phase and its milestones. Adjust to the program's structure and rules. Then project them onto each current cohort by year.
3. Map the program's yearly cycle. Use the "The program's yearly cycle" section below for admissions, orientation, events, evaluations, committees and grant reporting.
4. Add national dates. Use the "National dates" section below. Look up each date for the current year from its official source. If you can't look it up, enter the usual window and mark it "check."
5. Add owners and reminders. Use the "Owners and reminders" section below to set who is responsible and when reminders go out.
6. Check for clashes. Compare against both academic calendars, exam blocks, holidays and major religious observances. Flag clashes for the team to decide.
7. Build the calendar and its views. Use the "Building the calendar" section below for the master table, the views by month, cohort and owner, and a file to import into the team's calendar system.
8. Plan upkeep. Name the owner and set a yearly date to refresh national dates and roll cohorts forward.

## Rules that always apply

- Never invent a date. Use the program's dates, an official source or a clearly marked usual window to check.
- Name the source for every external date and the year it was checked.
- The program's rules come before anything in this skill.
- Keep individual students' progress out of shared calendars. Use cohort years in the calendar and keep individual tracking in a protected record.
- Avoid scheduling required events on major holidays and religious observances when possible. Offer remote or recorded options for program-wide events.
- Ask before deleting or moving an existing event. Someone may depend on it.

## What this skill does not do

It does not set program policy, approve students' timelines or replace the medical school and graduate school registrars' official calendars.

### National dates

Look up each one for the current year from its official source. These are usual windows only. Record the source and the date you checked.

| Event | Usual window | Official source |
|---|---|---|
| AMCAS application opens and submission begins | May to early June | AAMC AMCAS |
| AMCAS Choose Your Medical School tool dates: Plan to Enroll available, the date for narrowing acceptances, Commit to Enroll | February to May | AAMC Application and Acceptance Protocols for Applicants |
| NIH F30 fellowship standard due dates | April 8, August 8 and December 8 | NIH standard due dates and the current F30 parent announcement |
| NIH institutional training grant (T32) standard due dates | January 25, May 25 and September 25, or the dates in the program's announcement | NIH standard due dates and the current T32 announcement |
| NRSA stipend levels for the year | Usually announced in the spring | NIH Guide notice on NRSA levels |
| USMLE testing windows | Year round, with program timing | USMLE and NBME |
| ERAS opens and applications go to programs | June, then September | AAMC ERAS |
| Residency interview season | October to January | Specialty organizations |
| NRMP rank list deadline and Match Day | Late February to March | NRMP |
| ASCI, AAP and APSA Joint Meeting | Spring | The meeting's website |
| MD-PhD National Student Conference | Summer | The conference website |
| AAMC GREAT Group and MD-PhD Section meeting | Fall | AAMC |

Check whether the F30 announcement is the one for institutions with or without an NIH-funded dual-degree program. NIH issued separate parent announcements.

If a date can't be confirmed, enter the usual window, mark it "check" and add it to the upkeep list.

### Owners and reminders

Give every entry an owner and at least one reminder. These lead times come from experienced program teams. Adjust to the program.

| Item | First reminder | Second reminder |
|---|---|---|
| Committee meeting due | 8 weeks before, to the student and mentor | 2 weeks before, if not scheduled |
| Annual progress report or Individual Development Plan | 6 weeks before | 1 week before |
| Qualifying exam | 6 months before, to plan | 6 weeks before |
| F30 application | 6 months before, to plan with the mentor | Internal deadline, usually 4 to 6 weeks before NIH |
| Return to clinical training | 12 months before, to set the date | 6 months before, to register for rotations |
| Step exams | 6 months before | 6 weeks before |
| ERAS and letters | 6 months before ERAS opens | 6 weeks before letters are due |
| Admissions interview days | When dates are set, to faculty interviewers | 2 weeks before |
| Retreat and major events | 3 months before, save the date | 2 weeks before |
| T32 progress report | 3 months before, to gather data | 4 weeks before |
| Advisory committee meeting | 2 months before | 2 weeks before, with materials |

### Owners

Use roles, not personal names, so the calendar survives staff changes: program director, associate director, program administrator, admissions coordinator, student leadership.

### Building the calendar

### Master table

One row per entry, with these columns:

| Column | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| Event | A short, plain name |
| Date or window | A date, or a start and end for windows |
| Recurring | Yearly, each term or one time |
| Category | Admissions, students and community, advising and progress, program operations, grants or national |
| Audience | Who it is for, such as all students, a cohort, faculty or staff |
| Cohort | Entering year, if it applies to one cohort |
| Owner | A role |
| Reminders | When reminders go out and to whom |
| Source | Where the date comes from and when it was checked |
| Status | Confirmed, usual window to check, or clash to resolve |

### Views

- By month: what happens across the program this month and what reminders go out.
- By cohort: each entering class's milestones for the next 12 months.
- By owner: each role's tasks and reminders.
- Clashes: entries that overlap with exams, holidays or other required events.

### Importing into a calendar system

Offer a CSV the team can import. For Google Calendar use the columns Subject, Start Date, Start Time, End Date, End Time, All Day Event, Description and Location. For Outlook use its import format. Put the owner, audience and source in the Description. Include only confirmed dates in the import. Keep windows to check in the master table until they are confirmed.

### Checks before sharing

- Every entry has an owner and a source.
- No individual student names or progress in shared views.
- Clashes are listed for the team to decide.
- National dates were checked this year.
