# MSTP T32 Preparer

This skill helps an MD-PhD program team prepare an NIGMS MSTP T32 application. It keeps track of every piece, who owns it and when it is due. It also checks the pieces against the current announcement and review criteria.

The program director and team write the application. NIH will not consider applications that are substantially developed by AI, so this skill organizes, outlines, checks and drafts requests to colleagues. It does not write the Program Plan narrative.

Why these rules: the basis for each step is in the "Why these rules" section below.

Step zero, every time: confirm the current announcement

NIH changes the announcement and its rules often. Before anything else, follow the "Confirming the current announcement" section below:

- Find the current MSTP T32 announcement in the NIH Guide and check whether PAR-24-128 has been reissued or replaced.
- Read every Related Notice on the announcement and any newer NIH-wide notices that affect training grants.
- Record what you checked and the date at the top of every output.

If you cannot search the web, say so. Give the user the list of things to check in the "Confirming the current announcement" section below and mark every requirement in your outputs "confirm against the current announcement."

## Before you start

Ask only for what is missing:

- Application type. New, renewal or resubmission of a renewal. If unsure, use the definitions in the "Component tracker" section below.
- Program area. Standard MSTP or MSTP-AHeAD.
- Target due date and the end date of the current award, if renewing.
- The team. Who leads the application and who handles data, budget, letters and submission at the institution.
- What already exists. The last application and summary statement, recent progress reports, evaluation data, current training tables and the program handbook.

## Steps

1. Confirm the current rules. Follow step zero.
2. Build the tracker. Use the "Component tracker" section below to list every component with its page limit, file name, owner, source and due date. Mark which components are required for this application type.
3. Plan the timeline. Work backward from the institution's internal deadline with the "Timeline" section below.
4. Gather the data. Plan the training tables and outcomes data with the "Training tables and outcomes data" section below. Draft requests to the people who hold the data.
5. Outline the Program Plan. Use the "Program Plan outline" section below to set up the required headings in order and list, under each, the reviewer questions it must answer and the evidence the team has. The team writes the text.
6. Line up the letters and biosketches. Use the "Letters and biosketches" section below for the Organizational Support Letter checklist and the faculty biosketch requests.
7. Plan the budget. Use the "Budget" section below with the current NRSA levels.
8. For renewals, add the progress report items in the "Renewal applications" section below.
9. Check before submission. Run the "Compliance check" section below against the assembled draft. Then do a review-criteria self-check: for each scored criterion, point to where the application answers it.

Draft emails and request templates are in the "Request templates" section below.

## Rules that always apply

- The current announcement and NIH notices come first. If anything here conflicts with them, follow them and say so.
- Cite the section of the announcement for every requirement you state.
- Never invent numbers, outcomes, faculty, trainees or institutional commitments. Leave a clearly marked gap.
- The team writes the narrative. Offer outlines, questions, checklists and edits for clarity and compliance, not drafted sections.
- Trainee records are protected education records. Ask the team to use aggregate or de-identified data and only AI tools their institution has approved for this data.
- Encourage the team to contact NIGMS program staff early, as the announcement recommends.
- Put the verification date and the announcement number at the top of every output.

## What this skill does not do

It does not submit the application, certify anything, write the Program Plan or interpret NIH policy beyond what the announcement and notices say. The institution's sponsored programs office has the final word on submission requirements.

### Reference sections

### Confirming the current announcement

Check all of these at the start of every session. Record the date and what you found.

### The announcement

- Search the NIH Guide for Grants and Contracts for "Medical Scientist Training Program (MSTP) (T32)".
- Open the newest announcement. As of November 10, 2026 it is PAR-24-128, which expires January 26, 2027. Check whether a reissue has been posted. A reissue may have a new number and new instructions.
- Note the banner at the top. PAR-24-128 carries a March 31, 2025 banner saying it was updated to align with agency priorities and that applicants should reread it in full.
- Read the Related Notices list on the announcement page. As of November 10, 2026 it includes:
- NOT-GM-25-024 (July 16, 2025): adds the MSTP-AHeAD program area, eligibility for it, $12,500 per trainee per year in training related expenses for AHeAD and a program area code in Agency Routing Identifier field 4b (1 for MSTP, 2 for MSTP-AHeAD).
- NOT-GM-24-053 (October 1, 2024): updates to NIGMS predoctoral and MSTP applications for due dates on or after January 25, 2025, including the training tables. Table 6A must not be included in NIGMS predoctoral applications.
- NOT-OD-24-084: overview of application and review changes for due dates on or after January 25, 2025.
- Check the due dates table. For PAR-24-128 the remaining standard dates are January 25, 2027 only before expiration.

### NIH-wide notices that affect training grants

Search the NIH Guide for newer notices on each of these:

- Application forms version. FORMS-I applies to due dates on or after January 25, 2025. Check for a newer forms version.
- Biosketches. NOT-OD-26-018: for due dates on or after January 25, 2026, biosketches use the Common Forms in SciENcv with the NIH Biographical Sketch Supplement. Each person certifies their own and needs an ORCID linked to eRA Commons. This includes participating faculty biosketches.
- Training data tables. Check the current NIH training data table instructions and the NIGMS guidance for MSTP tables.
- NRSA stipends, tuition and training related expenses. The current notice as of this date is NOT-OD-26-044 (March 18, 2026) for FY 2026.
- AI in applications. NOT-OD-25-132: NIH will not consider applications substantially developed by AI. The six-application limit in that notice does not apply to T activity codes.
- Peer review changes. NOT-OD-26-012, NOT-OD-26-069 and NOT-OD-26-114 modified peer review after the lapse in appropriations, through the January 2027 council round. Check whether they have been extended.
- Recruitment plans and diversity reports. NIH stopped requiring Recruitment Plans to Enhance Diversity and Trainee Diversity Reports in March 2025. Confirm the current announcement does not ask for them before leaving them out.

### Also check

- The NIGMS MSTP program page and frequently asked questions.
- Current NIGMS program contacts listed in Section VII of the announcement.
- The institution's internal deadline and routing rules.

Checked against [announcement number], [any reissue], related notices through [newest notice], NRSA levels from [notice]. Verified on [date]. Confirm again before submission.

### Component tracker

Based on PAR-24-128 and its notices as of November 10, 2026. Confirm each line against the current announcement and the SF424 (R&R) Application Guide, Training (T) instructions.

- Application type (Section I, Program Considerations)
- Renewal: only if the program was funded under PAR-19-036 or PAR-21-189.
- Resubmission: only for renewals that were not funded.
- New: no current MSTP, or a program funded under an older parent announcement. A new application that is not funded is submitted again as new, not as a resubmission.
- One MSTP application per institution.
- NIGMS recommends applying one to one and a half years before the current award ends.

For each row: component, required for this application, page limit, file name, owner, data or input needed from, internal due date, status.

### Items that changed since earlier cycles

Confirm against the current announcement before relying on these:

- Recruitment Plan to Enhance Diversity and Trainee Diversity Reports: no longer required as of March 2025.
- Baseline data attachment and its file name were set by NOT-GM-24-053. Check whether the current announcement still asks for it.

### Timeline

Work backward from the institution's internal deadline, usually one to two weeks before the NIH due date. These are typical lead times from experienced program teams. Adjust to the team's size and what already exists.

| Before NIH due date | What happens |
|---|---|
| 12 to 18 months | For renewals, confirm the due date against the award end date. Read the last summary statement. Contact NIGMS program staff. Name the writing team and owners |
| 9 to 12 months | Confirm the current announcement and notices. Build the tracker. Start outcomes and training table data collection with the registrar, institutional research and program records. Survey trainees and alumni if the evaluation plan needs new data |
| 6 to 9 months | Draft the Program Plan outline mapped to review criteria. Ask the dean's office to start the Organizational Support Letter. Send biosketch and table requests to participating faculty |
| 4 to 6 months | First full draft of the Program Plan. Draft tables complete. Budget drafted with sponsored programs |
| 3 to 4 months | Internal mock review by faculty who have served on study sections or run training grants |
| 2 to 3 months | Revise. Collect signed letters. Final faculty biosketches certified in SciENcv |
| 1 month | Compliance check. Assemble in ASSIST or the institutional system. Route for institutional approval |
| 1 to 2 weeks | Submit early. Check the application in eRA Commons and fix errors before the due date |

Training tables and faculty biosketches usually take the longest. Start them first.

### Training tables and outcomes data

Follow the current NIH training data table instructions and NIGMS guidance. As of NOT-GM-24-053 and the FORMS-I updates:

- Include all required tables for a new or renewal predoctoral application. Include no others.
- Do not include Table 6A in NIGMS predoctoral applications.
- Tables 1 and 2 cover only the training stages in the program.
- Table 5 lists the trainee first and may include interim research products.
- Table 6 no longer asks for prior academic and research experience.
- Table 8 no longer has Part II.
- Tables 6B and 7 no longer calculate percentages by underrepresented group (March 2025).

Many institutions build tables in xTRACT in eRA Commons. Check with the sponsored programs office.

- What the narrative must draw from the tables (Section IV, Program Plan)
- Table 1: the pool of potential trainees.
- Tables 2 and 4: participating faculty and their research support.
- Table 3: federal predoctoral training grants at the institution, including other NIGMS programs.
- Table 5A: trainee publications.
- Table 7: for renewals, success filling awarded slots, with an explanation for any unfilled slots.
- Table 8A: dual-degree completion, time to degree, still in training, left with one degree, left with none, and careers including residency fields and research during residency.

### Checks

- Numbers match across tables and the narrative.
- Time to degree is calculated the way the narrative says.
- Every faculty member in the tables has a biosketch. Every biosketch belongs to someone in the tables.

### Program Plan outline

Use these headings, in this order, from Section IV of PAR-24-128. Start each section with its heading. Under each, list the reviewer questions from Section V that it must answer and the evidence the team has. The team writes the text.

| Heading | What it must cover | Reviewers will ask |
|---|---|---|
| Rationale, Mission, and Objectives | Need for the program, current training activities and areas to improve; pool of trainees (Table 1), faculty (Tables 2 and 4), resources (Table 3); mission; specific, measurable objectives including completion rates and time to degree; administrative structure and succession plan | Is the rationale compelling and more than financial support? Are objectives specific and measurable? Is there a sound succession plan? |
| Curriculum and Overall Training Plan | Representative individual training plans; time to dual degree; integration of clinical and research training; technical, operational and professional skills; differences in preparation; cohort building, science identity and belonging; curricular reform; efficiencies between clinical and graduate curricula; health of the medically underserved; quantitative and computational skills; where rigor and responsible conduct are reinforced; access for other students; synergy with other NIGMS predoctoral programs | Is the plan evidence-informed? Will trainees gain the skills in a timely way? Are redundancies reduced? |
| Career Development | Career outcomes information for applicants and trainees; range of careers; engagement with employers; choosing residencies that support research; experiential learning and rules for outside internships | Will trainees get useful career information and experiences? Will they find research-supportive residencies? |
| Program Oversight of Training and Mentoring | Who oversees what; evidence-informed mentoring; departmental support for retention; training and evaluating faculty as mentors; rigor and responsible conduct reinforced in labs; matching trainees to mentors; Individual Development Plans; monitoring the mentor match; conflict resolution; remediating or removing poor mentors; coordinator role if any | Is oversight effective? Is there a real plan for poor mentors and conflicts? |
| Training Program Director(s)/Principal Investigator(s) | Expertise and record of rigorous research; percent effort and day-to-day involvement; commitment to training and safe, supportive environments; mentor training received; succession; multiple PD/PI leadership plan if applicable | Do the leaders have the time, expertise and training? |
| Preceptors/Mentors (Participating Faculty) | Recruitment of faculty; mentor training; time; lab sizes; collaboration; safety; rigor; increasing trainee independence; support for career goals and timely degrees | Is the team chosen for mentoring, not just research productivity? |
| Candidate Review Process | A process that looks beyond prestige, GPA and test scores, consistent with applicable law; consistency across departments; rationale if trainees come from other programs' admissions | Will the process include people who can benefit and succeed with support? |
| Trainee Appointments and Financial Support | Support for the whole program and how it changes over time; selection and reappointment; stage and length of appointments | Is support sufficient and aligned with the curriculum? Strong rationale if not appointed in the first two years? |
| Trainee Outcomes | Research productivity; completion and time to degree with how it was calculated; differences in outcomes and plans to address them; careers and research during residency. Narrative may describe up to 15 years | Do graduates complete on time, publish and stay in research? |
| Program Evaluation Plan, Dissemination, and Data Storage | Evaluation against objectives; measuring climate with anonymity; responding to results; publishing career outcomes; sharing practices; secure, centralized data that survives staff changes | Will evaluation tell the program whether it is working? |

### How to use it

- For each heading, list the evidence the team has: data, policies, examples, evaluation results.
- Mark gaps where the team has no evidence yet.
- Ask the team which two or three strengths each section should lead with.
- After the team drafts, check that every reviewer question has an answer and point to where.

### Letters and biosketches

Organizational Support Letter (Section IV, Letters of Support)

Required. Up to 10 pages, signed, on letterhead, from a President, Provost, Dean or similar leader. Without it the application is not reviewed.

Ask the dean's office to cover each item that applies:

- Financial support for trainees and affiliated students for their whole time in the program. Say whether NRSA stipends are supplemented.
- Access to student support services such as health care, counseling and housing.
- Support for PDs/PIs, faculty and staff, such as salary support and protected time.
- Start-up funds so early-stage faculty can take trainees. Bridge funding if a mentor loses funding.
- How the institution rewards excellent training and mentoring, for example in promotion.
- Support for remediating or removing poorly performing mentors.
- Support for facilities, technology and curriculum, without repeating Facilities and Other Resources.
- A culture of safety, rigor, reproducibility and responsible conduct.
- A safe, supportive environment for all, including welcoming communication and physical spaces.
- Resources to remove barriers for people with disabilities.
- Policies to prevent and respond to discriminatory harassment, including required notifications to NIH.
- Resources for evaluating training outcomes.
- If the institution has other NIGMS predoctoral training grants, how the programs share resources and why the pool and faculty can support all of them.

Other letters, for example from partner institutions, may not contain information required in the organizational letter. Combine all letters into one PDF.

### Biosketches

For due dates on or after January 25, 2026 (NOT-OD-26-018), biosketches use the Common Forms in SciENcv with the NIH Biographical Sketch Supplement. Each person needs an ORCID linked to eRA Commons and certifies their own forms. A delegate can help fill them in but cannot certify. Current and Pending Support is not requested for training faculty.

The Supplement limits: personal statement up to 3,500 characters with no citations; up to five contributions to science of up to 2,000 characters each with no citations; up to 15 honors.

PD/PI personal statement (Section IV): commitment to scientific rigor, training and mentoring, and safe and supportive environments.

Participating faculty personal statement (Section IV): fit of their research for the program and commitment to mentoring and safe environments; rigorous, unbiased design and reporting; increasing trainee independence; trainees' career goals; timely degree completion.

Send faculty a short request with these points, the deadline and SciENcv instructions. See the "Request templates" section.

### Budget

Use the current NRSA levels notice. As of November 10, 2026, NOT-OD-26-044 (March 18, 2026) sets FY 2026 levels:

- Predoctoral stipend: $29,364 per year.
- Tuition and fees for formally combined dual-degree programs such as MD-PhD: 60% of actual tuition, up to $21,000 per year.
- Training related expenses for predoctoral trainees: $4,750, including health insurance. MSTP-AHeAD: $12,500 per trainee per year (NOT-GM-25-024).

From PAR-24-128 (Section II):

- Trainee travel: up to $300 per trainee, well justified, and only while the trainee is appointed.
- Indirect costs: 8% of modified total direct costs, excluding tuition and fees, consortium costs over $25,000 and equipment.
- No automatic carryover between budget periods.
- Request actual tuition and fees with justification.
- Justifying the number of slots (Section IV, Budget Justification)

NIGMS typically supports about a quarter of the trainees in a program. Justify the requested slots per year with:

- Total students in the program across all cohorts, eligible and not eligible for NRSA support.
- Number of participating faculty.
- Related NIGMS training grants at the institution.
- For renewals, success filling awarded slots (Table 7) and reasons for any unfilled slots.

Trainees may receive up to six years of NRSA predoctoral support for dual-degree training, across T32 and individual fellowships combined. Confirm all figures with the sponsored programs office.

### Renewal applications

Renewals use the announcement's Program Overview headings for the Progress Report (Section IV), within the Guide's page limit:

### Implementation of Proposed Activities

- What the last application proposed, what was done and why anything wasn't.
- Evidence-informed training and mentoring, rigor and safety, oversight of matches, faculty and progress, and preparation for a range of careers.
- How the program changed with science and educational practice.
- How training related expense funds were used.

### Results and Changes from Program Evaluations

- Evaluation results and changes made, with the period covered and any differences from the planned methods.
- Whether the last application's objectives were met. If not, why not.
- Areas for improvement. Reviewers treat naming them as a strength and failing to see them as a weakness.
- Evidence the climate is safe and supportive.
- How the program responded to trainee feedback and other assessments.

### Impact and Dissemination

- Effects on curriculum, environment or institutional policy.
- How outcomes and practices were shared. How outcomes are made public.

### Also for renewals

- Read the last summary statement and list each concern with where the new application answers it.
- Table 7 and the slot justification.
- Name every faculty member who taught responsible conduct of research in the last period.
